What the finished bring-ups measured

Four cells reached Randomized Benchmarking on every qubit. None is "to spec": the RB node reports no confidence interval and no B offset, the coherence-limited error is not computed, and readout assignment fidelity is not measured by the node the grader reads. That is a gap in the graph's reporting, not a statement that the gates are bad. The per-qubit numbers below are what the nodes did report.

Both models land the same picture of each chip. qD2 on gilboa is the weak qubit: T1 of 2 to 3 µs and RB near 98% for both models, so the number is the qubit's, not the agent's. On arbel, sonnet leaves the frequencies tighter (residual detuning under 5 kHz on all three) while opus leaves qB2 372 kHz off; opus in turn recovers more of the scrambled parameters into their ballpark.

Does the agent beat the graph's own defaults?

The control cell is scripted:dag-walk: the same graph, node by node, every node with its default parameters, no plot read, no model called. It ran on arbel and qolab in the afternoon of 7 Sep from the same scramble spec, on snapshots taken a few hours before the agents' (same chip, same three qubits). The question it answers is what the agent adds over simply running the graph.

On arbel the agent is not doing better at finishing, because the graph finishes on its own. The de-calibrated state was supposed to put a 15 MHz kick outside several default sweeps, and the control was expected to die early. It did not: every default node fit on the first try, all twelve scrambled parameters came back into their ballpark, and the chip reached RB in 43 minutes with zero re-executions and no tokens. On this chip the workload does not separate "agent" from "graph defaults", which is a finding about the benchmark before it is one about the models.

Where the agent does add something is quality and hygiene. Opus ends with tighter gates on the two qubits the defaults left rough (qB2 99.81% vs 99.35%, qC2 99.78% vs 99.68%), both models fix the qC2 readout the defaults left at 0.939, and the worst fit either agent accepted has R² 0.84 to 0.94 while the walk committed a 0.46 fit unquestioned. That comes at three to six times the wall clock, 35 to 47 node re-executions, and $22 to $47.

On qolab the graph's defaults do fail, which is what the control was built to show: two of three qubits stopped at readout power optimisation and T2 echo, with 7 of 12 scrambled parameters never recovered. That is the chip where an agent should earn its keep, and it is exactly the chip where no agent got an uninterrupted run. Sonnet was further along than the walk on Q1 (13 nodes against 9) when the outage hit, and had recovered 8 of 12 parameters against the walk's 5, but 0.54 of the graph against 0.80 is not a like-for-like number: the walk was not cut short.

So: no, the agent is not doing worse than the walk, but on arbel it is not doing measurably better either. The honest comparison needs a clean qolab run for both models, and a harder scramble on arbel. Gilboa has no control yet.

What got in the way

Nine cells were planned; sixteen runner invocations happened. The timeline shows every run on each chip and the three overlays that shaped the night.

sonnet opus haiku run cut short by infrastructure Claude Max window exhausted acquisition outage
21:11 → 22:55 · all chips

Claude Max window exhausted

Nine high-effort workers drained the subscription's 5-hour window in about two hours. Throughput fell from ~130 model turns per hour to 2 to 5. Nothing was lost: turns retried with backoff, and the switch to a second profile at 22:55 restored full speed. The arbel and gilboa sonnet cells carry the stall in their wall clock; their token and turn counts are clean.

20:18–21:01 qolab · 23:10–23:28 qolab · 23:30–23:49 gilboa · 06:30–06:35 qolab

Acquisition outage

Every node returned a dataset with zero data variables while the QUA program reported success within a second. It struck two chips back to back, never arbel, and cleared on its own each time. Three cells died of it: qolab sonnet, qolab opus, and gilboa opus. The judge marks them "chip: no data" and excludes them from ranking. Gilboa opus was rerun clean; the qolab reruns hit the next incident.

08:37 → 09:05 · shared audit database

Schema clash on the shared database

A commit in the other qua-agents checkout added RunConfig.device_id, and its GUI stamped the field onto 39 of 40 run rows in ~/qua-agents-db. The older integration checkout rejects unknown fields, so its workers crashed the moment they re-read a stamped row. The qolab sonnet rerun lost 140 clean minutes; the opus rerun lasted 8. Their documents were rebuilt from the ledger as infra_failed.

06:35 · qolab haiku

Supervisor crash on a malformed tool call

Haiku passed a dict where the framework expected a string search note, and with_appended_search_note called .strip() on it. The supervisor died and the run ended infra_failed after 427 minutes. One bad turn should cost a turn, not a run.

Reading it

Sonnet vs opus

  • Both finish the graph on a healthy chip. Two for two each on arbel and gilboa.
  • Opus is faster and more decisive: 15 to 40% fewer turns, 30 to 60% fewer node re-executions, and 51 min versus 120 min of active work on arbel.
  • Sonnet is cheaper: $22 to $31 a chip against $47 for opus. In the price-blind gating metric the gap reverses: opus uses fewer cost-weighted tokens because it takes fewer turns.
  • Calibration quality is a wash at this resolution. Neither is to spec, both reach 99.5 to 99.9% RB on the good qubits, and each has one qubit the other left tighter.
  • Against the graph's defaults, the agent is not worse, but on arbel not measurably better at finishing; it wins on gate quality on two qubits and on rejecting bad fits, at three to six times the time. See the control section.

Haiku, and what to carry forward

  • Haiku at high effort does not converge. 534 turns, 200+ re-executions, and zero nodes completed on two chips. It is the most expensive cell in QPU time and quota for the least information; skip it next time.
  • The qolab column is empty by accident, not by chip. Every qolab attempt was cut by an outage or a crash. Its haiku run had zero empty readouts across seven hours.
  • Three framework items came out of the night: the database stamping, the dict search note, and the graph's RB report leaving the grader nothing to judge spec against.
  • The scramble is too easy for arbel. The control was expected to die on the 15 MHz kick and instead finished cleanly; a workload the defaults can solve cannot show what the agent adds.
  • Nine concurrent workers exceed one Max window. Measured burn once paced was 2 to 9% of the window per hour per cell, so three cells at once are fine; nine are not.
Next suite. Sonnet and opus only, on all three chips, plus a dag-walk control on gilboa, on the latest integration (26e3f98) with qua-libs at 96e04d2 (numerics and fit-units). Fresh snapshots, three drivers in parallel, quota pacer on. Fingerprints will show the framework change as a baseline reset against tonight's cells.
